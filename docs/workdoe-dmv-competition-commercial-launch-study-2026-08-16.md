# Workdoe DMV Competition and Commercial Launch Study

Date: 2026-08-16  
Last updated: 2026-08-17  
Product: Workdoe, "a local Work Exchange"  
Geography: District of Columbia, suburban Maryland, and Northern Virginia  
Decision horizon: controlled DMV pilot through initial commercial launch

## Executive Decision

Workdoe should launch as a focused, trust-forward project exchange for small and medium local property work. It should not try to match the category breadth, paid acquisition budget, or national directory scale of Angi, Thumbtack, Yelp, Google, or Nextdoor.

The launch wedge is narrower:

1. Consumers create a sufficiently scoped project before contractors spend time responding.
2. Contractors browse real local work, see the commercial facts that determine fit, and submit a compact bid.
3. Each project accepts a small, visible number of qualified bids rather than being sold repeatedly as an unlimited shared lead.
4. Approximate location is public; exact address and direct contact stay private until the consumer approves a match.
5. License and insurance claims are separated from Workdoe verification. High-risk work is gated until the required verification operations exist.
6. Workdoe charges for useful software or a successful transaction, not for an unresponsive name and phone number.

This positioning is supported by three findings:

- The large marketplaces increasingly optimize guided intake, response speed, reviews, availability, and fit. Workdoe needs those basics, but it does not need their national complexity.
- Pay-per-lead remains a major revenue model. It is also the clearest source of contractor distrust when the same request reaches several businesses or never converts.
- The DMV is large enough for a focused market. The Census Bureau estimates 6,465,724 residents in the Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metropolitan area in July 2025, while BLS reports an annual average of 171,900 mining, logging, and construction payroll jobs in 2025. Those figures establish a substantial regional demand and supply base, but they are not a Workdoe revenue forecast.

## Research Standard

This study uses public sources only: official product and help pages, regulator guidance, government data, public SEC filings, and publicly available research. It does not use competitor source code, confidential documents, leaked material, private APIs, or protected visual assets.

Studying a public workflow does not make that workflow or a competitor's site "open source." Workdoe may adopt general marketplace patterns such as structured intake, availability controls, verified reviews, and private messaging. It must not copy protected code, branded copy, illustrations, interface artwork, or nonpublic implementation details.

Vendor-reported statistics are labeled as vendor claims. Public forum comments are treated as anecdotal signals, not representative market evidence. Product and legal statements were checked against sources available on the study date.

## Market Structure

The competition falls into five models.

| Model | Representative products | Primary consumer value | Primary contractor cost | Main structural risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Guided matching | Thumbtack, Angi, Porch, Bark | Describe a need and receive matches | Lead, credit, advertising, or membership fees | A lead can be monetized before a contractor wins work |
| Scheduled labor | Taskrabbit, Angi Services | Choose a worker, time, and price for a defined task | Registration or transaction economics; consumer platform fees | Works best for standardized scopes, less well for uncertain projects |
| Local search and reputation | Google Local Services Ads, Yelp | Search high-intent local providers and compare reputation | Pay per lead, bid, or advertising subscription | Ranking spend can outrun fit; reputation cold start is difficult |
| Neighborhood discovery | Nextdoor, Craigslist, Facebook Groups | Local reach, direct contact, low friction | Posting fees or ads; often free organic reach | Weak scoping, inconsistent identity, and scam exposure |
| Professional portfolio and operations | Houzz Pro | Discover high-consideration design/build providers; manage the sales pipeline | Subscription and advertising | Too heavy for a small job pilot, but strong after lead qualification |

Workdoe's intended model is a hybrid of a structured local project board and controlled request-to-match. It is not an employment agency, a general classifieds site, or initially a contractor-of-record/payment marketplace.

## Competitor Deep Dives

### Thumbtack

**Current direction.** Thumbtack's 2024 fact sheet described about 500 service categories and 80 million projects started. Its 2026 product announcement says it is testing an issue-first experience where a homeowner explains a problem with text, photos, or voice and receives a curated list of local pros with fit explanations based on expertise, needs, ratings, and availability.

**Strengths.** Rich category questions improve scope; profiles and reviews support comparison; instant booking works for standardized work; scheduling an estimate handles uncertain scopes.

**Commercial pressure.** Public Thumbtack material indicates there are no subscription fees for the basic lead product and that pros configure targeting, maximum lead prices, and budgets. Exact prices vary. Public contractor forums repeatedly complain about nonresponse and several pros paying around the same request. These comments are anecdotal, but the pattern is consistent enough to test directly during interviews.

**Adopt.** Issue-first language, photos, availability, explicit fit reasons, and separate flows for fixed tasks versus estimate-required work.

**Avoid.** Black-box categorization as the source of truth, automatic paid distribution to a large pool, or a charge before Workdoe has evidence of a meaningful consumer-pro interaction.

### Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Handy/Angi Services

**Current model.** Angi publicly describes consumer search, quote requests, and book-and-pay services. Its FAQ says revenue comes from service professionals, consumer memberships, and Angi Services. Angi's 2025 Form 10-K reports $587.1 million of U.S. lead revenue, 57 percent of consolidated revenue, alongside advertising, services, and membership revenue.

**Trust.** Angi says an owner, principal, or relevant manager of an Angi Approved pro is background checked, subject to stated exceptions, and the pro attests to applicable licenses. The wording matters: an identity/background check is not a skills guarantee and a license attestation is not the same as live verification.

**Strengths.** Category depth, cost guidance, broad distribution, and several ways for a consumer to move from research to purchase.

**Weaknesses.** The mix of directory, shared lead, advertisement, and managed-service relationships can make it hard for a user to understand who selected whom, who holds the contract, and what a badge means.

**Adopt.** Cost education, profile quality standards, and a clear complaint/removal workflow.

**Avoid.** Layering several commercial relationships into the pilot. Every Workdoe project should state whether it is a request for bids, an approved match, or later a platform-supported booking.

### Taskrabbit

**Model.** Taskrabbit is the closest reference for bounded work. Taskers choose their own rates, clients select a Tasker, and the platform supports chat, scheduling, invoicing, and payment. Taskrabbit states that U.S. Taskers undergo identity and criminal background screening. Clients pay platform fees in addition to the Tasker's rate, and the Tasker retains the hourly rate set by the Tasker plus tips.

**Strengths.** A consumer sees rate, availability, experience, reviews, and a concrete worker. The platform is strongest when scope and duration can be estimated without a site visit.

**Limit.** Remodeling, hidden damage, permit work, and multi-trade projects are not naturally hourly catalog tasks.

**Adopt.** Availability controls, project-specific chat, explicit cancellation states, and a distinction between small bookable work and quote-required work.

**Avoid.** Forcing all services into hourly pricing or implying that platform screening certifies trade competence.

### Airtasker

**Model.** Airtasker is the closest public structural comparison to Workdoe. A
consumer posts a task with a description, location, date, and budget; Taskers
submit a price and short offer; the consumer compares profiles, ratings,
completion rate, and reviews. Private messaging opens after an offer is accepted,
and the agreed amount is funded before work. Airtasker's U.S. support pages list a
consumer connection fee from $4.90 to $49.50 based on task price, a separate Tasker
service fee, and an optional $59 annual consumer membership that waives connection
fees. These are current public terms, not a recommendation for Workdoe pricing.

**Strengths.** The public job board makes demand tangible to workers; an explicit
budget makes an offer concrete; transaction-linked reviews and completion rates
create useful evidence; the accepted offer produces a clear contract state; funded
payment reduces collection risk.

**Weaknesses.** Public pre-assignment questions can expose project or personal
details, an unrestricted offer pool can create noise and price pressure, and
payments introduce KYC, refunds, disputes, tax, chargeback, and worker-classification
operations. Airtasker's own terms distinguish platform, task contract, contractor
service fee, and consumer connection fee because those relationships are legally
and operationally consequential.

**Adopt.** A visible project board, compact project-specific offers, profile and
verified-completion evidence, one accepted match, and private post-approval
messaging. Workdoe's four-bid cap is a deliberate improvement hypothesis that must
be measured rather than assumed.

**Avoid.** Public negotiation, unlimited offers, collecting funds before Workdoe
can support the resulting obligations, and suggesting that a platform identity or
background check proves trade competence.

### Google Local Services Ads

**Model.** Google places screened local businesses in high-intent search results. Advertisers choose service areas and budgets and pay for valid calls, messages, or other defined leads. Prices vary by location, category, lead type, and bidding mode.

**Trust.** Google's screening varies by category and region and may include license, insurance, business registration, and background checks. In 2025 Google began replacing prior badges with Google Verified and discontinued the former money-back guarantee for newly eligible work after December 7, 2025.

**Strengths.** Strong intent capture, category-specific verification, transparent budget controls, and direct consumer choice of a profile.

**Weaknesses.** Contractors still buy a lead rather than a completed job; ranking includes bid and response behavior; Workdoe cannot outspend Google on search acquisition.

**Adopt.** Verification components, profile transparency, response measurement, and direct links to authoritative license records.

**Avoid.** A bidding auction for organic Workdoe eligibility. Paid promotion must be clearly marked and must never manufacture a trust badge.

### Yelp

**Model.** Yelp combines free business profiles and reviews with Request a Quote, advertising, and page upgrades. Yelp says quote forms can ask category-specific project questions and consumers may choose to send the request to additional businesses. Its current pricing page lists Yelp Ads from $150 per month and an Upgrade Package at $180 per month.

**Strengths.** Durable business identity, reviews, project photos, search demand, and a free profile baseline.

**Weaknesses.** A strong existing reputation graph makes entry difficult for new firms, and quote distribution can expand beyond the initially selected business.

**Adopt.** Contractor-owned profile, service area, photos, clear quote action, and verified-completion review eligibility.

**Avoid.** Preselected distribution to competitors or paid page treatment that looks like an independent quality ranking.

### Houzz Pro

**Model.** Houzz combines visual discovery with subscription software for lead capture, CRM, meetings, estimates, proposals, schedules, and payments. Houzz describes its lead-generation model as subscription-based instead of pay-per-lead and says introductions are not broadcast to multiple pros.

**Strengths.** Portfolios fit high-consideration remodels, and the contractor receives useful operating software beyond acquisition.

**Weaknesses.** The workflow is too complex for Workdoe's initial lawn, cleaning, hauling, mounting, and pressure-washing jobs.

**Adopt.** Subscription value tied to saved service areas, bid templates, job pipeline, calendar, portfolio, and analytics.

**Avoid.** Building a full contractor ERP before Workdoe proves local liquidity.

### Porch and Bark

**Model.** Both are explicit lead marketplaces. Porch lets a pro buy individual leads or use a monthly budget and publishes detailed credit rules. Bark uses credits; its June 2026 help page lists a standard price of $2.35 per credit while each lead consumes a variable number of credits. Bark separately documents that a customer may receive up to five professional responses and that professionals can see the response count before deciding whether to respond.

**Operational lesson.** Their policies define bad contact data, duplicates, wrong categories, stale requests, nonresponse, and customers unable to authorize work. That is a ready-made quality taxonomy for Workdoe's moderation and analytics, even if Workdoe does not sell leads.

**Adopt.** Capture cancellation reason, contactability, authorization to hire, time-to-contact, wrong-category reports, and geographic mismatch.

**Avoid.** Making contractors learn a refund policy before they can understand the expected cost of winning work.

### Category Specialists: Lawn, Cleaning, and Moving

General marketplaces show how users discover providers. Specialist products show
how a category changes once the service is repeated, scheduled, routed, priced, and
verified.

**GreenPal versus LawnStarter.** GreenPal is a direct lawn-care bid marketplace:
the consumer creates a lawn request, can receive up to five bids, chooses a company,
and can schedule recurring service. Current public pages say consumers do not pay a
platform fee, vendors do not pay to submit a bid, and GreenPal takes a 5 percent
commission on work. LawnStarter uses the opposite allocation model for standard
mowing: it measures the property, generates an instant price, acquires the consumer,
and presents a recurring paid job to nearby providers to accept. LawnStarter says
providers see their pay before acceptance, pay no lead fee, and receive weekly
payment; its current consumer material describes delayed charging, a service
guarantee, and a $3.99 trust/safety fee per visit. Company counts, reviews, payout
figures, and performance claims are vendor-reported.

The Workdoe lesson is not to pick one mechanism globally. A unique yard cleanup or
landscape project can use bounded bids. A measured, recurring mow may eventually be
better as a priced route offer. For either path, recurrence, weather rescheduling,
arrival/completion evidence, scope changes such as overgrowth, and the ability to
keep a reliable provider are core product states.

**Homeaglow.** Homeaglow's Washington, DC pages foreground the individual cleaner:
the consumer sees profile, hourly rate, availability, completed cleanings, service
hours, and reviews, then can rebook that person. Public pricing pages describe a
two-hour minimum, a booking fee starting at 5 percent, and an optional monthly
membership for discounted hourly rates. The company says cleaners set rates and
receive cleaning fees and tips. The most transferable pattern is continuity: after
a successful first clean, forcing the household and cleaner back through a fresh
competitive bid is worse than a consented rebooking path.

**Dolly and Lugg.** Both operate in the Washington region and treat moving as
logistics. Their public flows ask what is moving, origin/destination, time, labor,
and vehicle capacity; they advertise upfront or guaranteed pricing, vetted or
background-checked workers, scheduling, messaging, and tracking. Lugg also surfaces
certificate-of-insurance support for apartment moves. Workdoe should therefore keep
vehicle transport, commercial hauling, and building-access moves in manual review
until it can represent vehicle class, crew size, stairs/elevators, parking, COI,
restricted items, disposal destination, live status, and damage claims.

### Contractor Operating Software

Jobber, Housecall Pro, Joist, and ServiceTitan are not consumer lead boards, but
they compete for the same contractor's software budget and attention. Jobber and
Housecall Pro cover the job lifecycle from request and quote through scheduling,
crew communication, invoice, payment, and review collection. Joist is a narrower,
lower-cost estimate/invoice/payment product for small contractors. ServiceTitan is
an operations platform for larger residential, commercial, and specialty trade
businesses.

Workdoe should not reproduce a field-service suite. Its commercial workspace must
earn a subscription by reducing work unique to this marketplace: saved fit filters,
lead alerts, reusable bid language, bid follow-up, match history, verified Workdoe
portfolio evidence, and a simple export or integration path into tools contractors
already use. Pricing must reflect that narrow value. Current public entry points
range from Joist plans in the low tens of dollars per month to Housecall Pro's
single-user annual-billing entry plan at $59 per month; larger operating systems go
well beyond that. Workdoe cannot charge full field-service-software prices for a
lead inbox alone.

### Nextdoor

**Model.** Nextdoor's free business pages collect recommendations and messages, while paid ads target ZIP codes or a radius. Nextdoor claims more than 100 million verified users and 4.1 million claimed business pages. Those are vendor claims, useful for scale context but not independent evidence.

**Strengths.** Local identity, neighbor recommendations, service-area targeting, and repeat word of mouth.

**Weaknesses.** Feed posts are less structured than projects, and neighborhood identity can create privacy and fairness concerns if copied too literally.

**Adopt.** Community-level reputation, repeat local relationships, and geographic relevance.

**Avoid.** Publishing exact household identity or allowing neighborhood popularity to substitute for licensing and verified work history.

### Craigslist and Facebook Groups

**Model.** Craigslist uses local categories and a deliberately short post flow. Current U.S. fees are $5 for service posts and $3-$10 for gigs. Meta's current public help page says services, including house cleaning, cannot be listed on Facebook Marketplace. The relevant substitute for Workdoe is therefore local Facebook Groups and community posts, not Marketplace's item-listing product.

**Strengths.** Fast, familiar, local, and low-cost.

**Weaknesses.** Scope and identity are inconsistent, conversations leave the platform quickly, moderation evidence is limited, and professional qualifications are hard to compare.

**Adopt.** Low-friction browsing, concise project cards, chronological freshness, and a simple one-time consumer boost only after the free marketplace works.

**Avoid.** Public contact details, exact addresses, unstructured catch-all categories, anonymous fulfillment, and unverifiable claims.

### Homes.com as an Adjacent Pattern

Homes.com is not a direct contractor marketplace. It is a residential property
discovery and agent-marketing marketplace. Homes.com says its consumer property
inquiries connect to the listing agent, while its agent directory can be searched by
location and specialty. Its agent membership promotes listings and profiles, and
CoStar's 2025 Form 10-K describes subscription memberships and single-listing
promotion as the core Homes.com products. CoStar reported that Homes.com monetization
began in February 2024; its 2026 proxy reported 35,000 agent subscribers and more than
$100 million in annualized run-rate revenue. These are company-reported figures, not
independent market estimates.

**Strengths.** The property itself anchors the inquiry; neighborhood and property
context reduce ambiguity; the consumer can see the responsible professional; MLS-linked
identity makes an agent profile more durable than a disposable lead form; members receive
visibility and analytics in addition to an inquiry.

**Limits for Workdoe.** Real-estate listings already have standardized records and a
single listing representative. Small property jobs often have no authoritative listing,
may be posted by a renter, small business, HOA, or property manager, and may need several
qualified contractors to compare scope and price. Workdoe must not collect MLS data,
infer ownership, or expose a street address merely to imitate this model.

**Adopt.** Ask for a coarse project setting such as house, apartment/condo, business
space, shared building area, or outdoor area. Keep one accountable consumer owner per
project, show the contractor exactly why the lead fits, cap the response pool, and later
sell useful storefront/analytics tools instead of obscuring who receives the inquiry.

**Avoid.** Property-listing aggregation, referral claims about an owner's agent,
pay-to-win trust labels, exact-address publication, and advertising that looks like
organic qualification.

## Competition-to-Launch Decision Matrix

This matrix translates competitor observations into Workdoe release decisions. "Implemented"
means the capability exists in the local reference application and Cloudflare Worker code; it
does not mean the production account, operator process, or user behavior has been proven.

| Marketplace capability | Current public evidence | Workdoe state on 2026-08-17 | Commercial decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem-first, structured intake | Thumbtack is moving from category search toward text/photo/voice issue intake; Airtasker and category specialists require a brief, location, date, and price signal | Six-step deterministic intake, canonical service families, photos, project setting, desired date, and budget are implemented | Keep canonical categories as the source of truth; validate the questions against 50 real project descriptions before adding semantic assistance |
| Visible local demand | Airtasker exposes a task board; Craigslist exposes chronological local posts; GreenPal turns an address brief into bids | Public list/map and approximate project locations are implemented | Make real versus demonstration demand unmistakable and measure unique eligible projects, not page views |
| Controlled provider competition | Airtasker uses offers; GreenPal states up to five bids; Angi recommends comparing quotes; lead marketplaces may sell one request several times | Four slots and a seven-day deadline are visible and enforced by the database write | Treat four as a hypothesis; interview contractors and monitor qualified bid count, cap fill, and repeat participation |
| Availability and route fit | Taskrabbit foregrounds availability; lawn platforms use route proximity; Uber demonstrates that nearest is not always operationally best | Canonical service zones, coarse availability, saved lead view, and deterministic fit reasons are implemented | Add freshness handling and travel constraints before ranking; do not rank by fastest click or hidden paid weight |
| Consumer choice and private contact | Taskrabbit and Homeaglow let the consumer select a named provider; Airtasker opens private messaging after assignment | Compact bids, consumer approval, and post-approval private messages are implemented | Preserve one accountable match state and do not release exact address/contact before approval |
| Precise trust labels | Taskrabbit describes the scope of its screening; Angi distinguishes background checks, attestations, and self-reported credentials; Google varies checks by category | A source-checked contractor credential ledger exists, but its live operator recheck process is unproven | Publish atomic facts and dates only; never collapse email, identity, license, insurance, and work history into one "verified" promise |

### Falsifiable pilot hypotheses

These hypotheses prevent the competitor study from becoming a list of attractive features.
Each must be accepted or rejected with Workdoe data and interviews.

1. **Scope hypothesis.** At least 70 percent of published real projects in an active service-zone cell will receive one eligible bid within 24 hours without an off-platform clarification call.
2. **Fair-competition hypothesis.** Showing a four-bid maximum and remaining slots will produce a median of two to four qualified bids while preserving contractor participation across four separate weeks.
3. **Privacy hypothesis.** City/ZIP-level location, approximate coordinates, project setting, photos, and access details will be sufficient to bid; exact-address requests before approval should be uncommon and reportable.
4. **Continuity hypothesis.** After a verified cleaning match, consumers with another need will prefer rebooking the selected contractor or reusing a template over starting an unrelated open auction.
5. **No-payment hypothesis.** A controlled beta can prove matching and fulfillment while payment remains directly between participants; approval-to-completion abandonment will show when payment support becomes material.
6. **Workspace-value hypothesis.** Active contractors will value route filters, availability, templates, verified history, and pipeline reminders more than paid lead priority.
7. **Zone-density hypothesis.** A service-zone cell with at least three eligible contractors and one backup can sustain the response promise without broad incentives; if not, recruitment and scope must change before demand spend.

### Commercial boundary and regulatory watch

The public product patterns do not resolve Workdoe's legal role. The IRS describes digital
platforms as businesses matching workers' services with customers and directs operators to
classify workers and review payment-reporting duties. The Department of Labor announced new
independent-contractor rulemaking in February 2026, so the governing federal analysis is an
active legal watch item. Labels in the Terms do not override the actual level of behavioral,
financial, scheduling, pricing, and performance control.

Before Workdoe sets prices, assigns contractors, mandates schedules, processes funds, offers a
performance guarantee, or adjudicates workmanship, qualified counsel should review worker
classification, employment-service licensing, marketplace tax reporting, principal/agent
presentation, insurance, and category-specific consumer-contract duties in DC, Maryland, and
Virginia. The free request-to-match beta still needs this review, but avoiding managed
fulfillment and payment keeps the initial role narrower and easier to describe truthfully.

## DMV Opportunity

### Regional scale

- The Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 table estimates 6,465,724 residents in the Washington-Arlington-Alexandria metro area as of July 1, 2025.
- A narrower initial core of DC, Montgomery County, Prince George's County, Fairfax County, Arlington County, and Alexandria contained about 4.31 million residents using July 2024 QuickFacts estimates. This is an arithmetic sum of six jurisdictions, not an official market definition.
- BLS reports 171,900 annual-average mining, logging, and construction payroll jobs in the metro in 2025, up from 166,100 in 2024. The broader category is not the same as addressable Workdoe contractors, but it demonstrates substantial supply.
- BLS counted 87,050 construction and extraction occupations in May 2024 and 18,060 construction laborers in the metro.
- Nationally, the public Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies estimated owner-occupied improvement and repair spending at $509 billion for 2025 and described a fragmented industry with skilled-labor shortages. This is national context, not a DMV TAM.

QuickFacts also helps distinguish two different pilot demand shapes. Fairfax County
reports 414,101 households and a 68.6 percent owner-occupied rate, while Montgomery
County reports 389,210 households and a 65.3 percent owner-occupied rate. Multiplying
those published measures yields roughly 538,000 owner-occupied households across the
two counties. DC and Arlington report 324,491 and 112,027 households with owner rates
near 41 percent, which points to a much larger renter share and a plausible concentration
of moving, turnover cleaning, assembly, and small-space work. These arithmetic estimates
are segmentation signals, not addressable-customer or revenue forecasts.

Workdoe should not publish a dollar TAM by multiplying national spend per household by DMV households. That would ignore renter/owner mix, project size, commercial work, platform category exclusions, and adoption. A defensible market model requires observed local project volume and bid conversion from the pilot.

### Geography is a product constraint

The term DMV hides three regulatory systems and several practical service zones. A contractor may accept work in Arlington but not Prince George's County because of travel time, tolls, parking, licensing, or crew logistics. ZIP radius alone is insufficient.

Contractor profiles should therefore store:

- home base or service-area origin privately;
- accepted jurisdictions and ZIP clusters;
- maximum travel time or distance;
- bridge/toll willingness;
- parking/urban-access constraints;
- service-specific radius, since mowing and roofing have different economics;
- schedule windows and urgent-work availability.

The consumer sees service availability, not the contractor's private home address.

### Seasonality

NOAA and the National Weather Service publish monthly normals for Reagan National, Dulles, Baltimore, and other regional stations, including precipitation and snowfall. Workdoe should use those public normals to schedule merchandising and supply outreach, not to dynamically reclassify projects.

Practical sequence:

| Season | Demand emphasis | Operational preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late winter | Interior cleaning, mounting, drywall, painting, minor repairs | Recruit indoor-service supply; prepare spring waitlists |
| Spring | Yard cleanup, mowing, gutters, pressure washing, moving | Expand outdoor supply before first demand spike |
| Summer | Lawn, exterior cleaning, moving, decks/fences, painting | Watch heat, storm, and schedule reliability |
| Fall | Leaves, gutters, drainage, weatherization, hauling | Promote recurring maintenance and bundled property checklists |
| Winter events | Snow removal and urgent exterior cleanup | Open only where verified supply and clear weather cancellation rules exist |

Weather response work has elevated fraud, safety, price, and insurance risk. It should not be opened as an unmoderated emergency category.

## DMV License and Safety Design

This is product research, not legal advice. Workdoe needs qualified counsel before unrestricted launch.

### District of Columbia

DC's Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection says anyone engaging in business activity in the District must hold the license applicable to the activity. Home Improvement Contractors have specific insurance, contract, criminal-history, surety bond, tax, and business requirements. DLCP also says subcontractors may need their own Basic Business License. Consumers can verify businesses through SCOUT, and the Department of Buildings publishes a contractor rating system based on permit and inspection performance.

**Product requirement:** store the asserted DC license type and number, verification timestamp, source link, and result. Never display "DC verified" when Workdoe has only received a self-entered number.

### Maryland

The Maryland Home Improvement Commission licenses and regulates home improvement contractors and salespersons. Maryland says only an MHIC-licensed contractor may contract directly with a homeowner for covered home improvement work. Its current public page says that, effective June 1, 2024, Maryland home improvement contractors must maintain at least $500,000 in general liability insurance. Maryland's definition reaches many tasks that a consumer may casually call yard or exterior work, including listed landscaping, fencing, gutters, painting, patios, paving, roofing, and similar residential improvements. Maryland's Guaranty Fund protections apply to licensed contractors and covered work.

**Product requirement:** for Maryland home improvement categories, require an MHIC number before the contractor can bid, verify status against the official lookup, and display the legal business/license name. Preserve the lookup timestamp because status changes.

### Virginia

Virginia licenses contractor businesses by class and classification/specialty. As of the July 29, 2026 Code of Virginia page, Class C covers projects over $1,000 and below $30,000 and specified annual volume, Class B covers $30,000 to below $150,000 and specified annual volume, and Class A covers $150,000 or more and specified annual volume. Electrical, plumbing, and HVAC contractor licensing requires the applicable master tradesman condition.

The state threshold is not the entire local rule. Fairfax County says a contractor
performing home-improvement work below the state Class C threshold must still obtain a
Fairfax County Home Improvement Contractor license. Workdoe therefore cannot treat a
budget below $1,000 as a universal Virginia exemption.

**Product requirement:** capture project budget range and service specialty before bid eligibility so the platform can warn when a license class or specialty may be relevant. The platform should not make the final legal determination.

### Federal lead-safe work

EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule applies to paid work that disturbs painted surfaces in most pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities, subject to specific thresholds and exceptions. It can cover painters, plumbers, carpenters, electricians, property managers, and other firms.

**Product requirement:** ask whether the property was built before 1978 for paint-disturbing scopes and surface the EPA certification question. Do not publish the exact property year or address in the open lead.

## Workdoe Product Position

### Consumer promise

"Post one clear local project, keep your exact address private, and compare a small set of relevant bids."

The consumer should not need to understand lead markets. The interface should answer:

- What kind of work is this?
- What details and photos will prevent wasted visits?
- Which area is safe to reveal now?
- When and at what budget does the work need to happen?
- How many contractors may bid?
- What has Workdoe actually verified?

### Contractor promise

"See the scope before you spend time, choose work that fits your route and skills, and know the competition rules."

Every lead card should eventually show:

- service and scope summary;
- approximate area and estimated travel fit;
- desired timing and budget signal;
- photo count and access constraints;
- consumer readiness/completeness;
- bid slots used and bid deadline;
- verification requirements;
- whether the project needs an estimate or can be priced directly;
- no direct contact until match approval.

**Pilot policy implemented.** Workdoe uses four mini-bid slots, not Bark's five,
as a DMV pilot hypothesis aligned with the target median of two to four qualified
responses. Each project opens for seven calendar days. Both sides see usage and
the close time; rejected bids still count; expired non-full windows can be
extended seven days by the owning consumer. There is no paid priority or opaque
rank boost. The cap and deadline are enforced by the same conditional database
write that creates a bid, so the interface cannot promise a slot that the data
layer has already filled.

**Consumer selection implemented, 2026-08-17.** The protected project view now
places up to four pending offers in received order and repeats the same price,
timeline, availability, source-check count, mutually verified Workdoe history,
and clearly qualified self-reported provider facts for each contractor. It does
not calculate a score, identify a recommended or cheapest winner, sell order,
or expose contact and credential identifiers. The summary links to the full
offer and profile; approval remains a deliberate action on the full offer. This
follows the FTC's advice to compare multiple estimates by work description,
completion timing, and price without automatically choosing the lowest bidder,
and uses the GOV.UK summary-list principle of repeating consistent labeled facts
for multiple people or organizations.

### Differentiation

Workdoe is not "dating for contractors" in public product copy. The interaction can feel quick and reciprocal, but commercial work requires enough scope, documentation, and accountability to be useful. The public differentiation is:

- local DMV density;
- structured, inspectable project scopes;
- limited bid competition;
- privacy before approval;
- jurisdiction-aware contractor verification;
- repeat work history based on verified Workdoe projects;
- simple, disclosed economics.

### Defensibility and non-goals

Workdoe will not be defensible because it has a map, messaging, or a broad service
directory. Each is common. The plausible DMV advantage is the operating dataset and
trust system created by disciplined local execution:

- canonical service and jurisdiction activation records;
- observed scope fields that predict whether a contractor can bid without a visit;
- contractor service-zone and route preferences;
- visible bid capacity and response reliability;
- controlled close reasons and two-sided completion evidence;
- exact records of which license, registration, or insurance check was performed;
- repeat relationships that preserve consumer choice and contractor value.

Do not attempt to win through a national category count, opaque recommendations,
unrestricted contractor signups, generic AI-generated scopes, or a full contractor
CRM. Workdoe should make the local exchange more inspectable and economical, then
integrate with the operating tools a contractor already trusts.

## Matching Without a Black Box

The first production matcher should be deterministic and explainable. A contractor is eligible when all required rules pass:

1. Account is active and contractor role is fixed.
2. Canonical service capability matches the project.
3. Contractor accepts the jurisdiction/service area.
4. Budget and minimum-job preferences do not conflict.
5. Availability overlaps the desired window.
6. Required license/insurance checks are current for gated services.
7. Contractor is not blocked by the consumer or moderation policy.
8. Bid cap has not been reached.

Eligible results can then be ranked by visible factors such as service fit, travel fit, response reliability, recent availability, and verified completed work. Each ranking reason should be available in plain language. Paid promotion may occupy a clearly labeled placement but must not override license or safety eligibility.

Vector search can later retrieve conceptually related projects or services, but D1 remains the source of truth for category, eligibility, billing, and moderation.

Uber's H3 geospatial index is available under Apache-2.0 and could later support coarse
service-cell analytics or proximity search. It is not necessary for the first two-zone pilot,
where named service zones are easier for operators and contractors to inspect. If adopted,
H3 cells should be derived from protected coordinates, use a privacy-reviewed resolution,
and never replace the exact-address permission boundary. Open-source H3 is a reusable
technology component; it is not permission to copy Uber's marketplace code, ranking, copy,
or product assets.

## Monetization Recommendation

### What the market demonstrates

- Craigslist proves one-time low posting fees can support a direct classifieds experience.
- Google, Angi, Thumbtack, Porch, and Bark demonstrate strong willingness among providers to pay for demand, but also expose the quality/refund problem of monetizing before a completed sale.
- Yelp combines free identity with paid visibility.
- Houzz demonstrates subscription value when software helps the contractor operate, not just acquire.
- Airtasker, Taskrabbit, and Uber demonstrate transaction-linked platform fees when the platform controls enough of assignment, payment, support, and marketplace reliability to justify them.
- Homeaglow demonstrates consumer membership tied to repeat category savings; GreenPal and LawnStarter show two different provider economics for recurring lawn work. None should be copied before Workdoe measures its own repeat behavior and operating cost.

### Recommended sequence

**Controlled pilot: free.** Do not introduce price while measuring whether a clear project can consistently produce qualified responses.

**Commercial beta: contractor Pro workspace.** Test a monthly subscription only after free contractors can still win work. Candidate paid tools:

- instant alerts and saved service-area filters;
- reusable bid templates and follow-up reminders;
- calendar and availability controls;
- portfolio capacity and verified work history;
- simple pipeline and conversion analytics;
- team seats for a small local business.

**Later: successful-match fee.** Consider a fixed disclosed fee when a consumer accepts a bid, with a cancellation/refund policy. Do this only after Workdoe can observe the transaction state and support disputes.

**Optional: consumer project boost.** A one-time, clearly labeled placement can be tested after organic liquidity is healthy. It must not imply urgency, quality, or verification.

### Explicitly reject for the pilot

- Charging several contractors for the same incomplete request.
- Credits that expire.
- Undisclosed dynamic lead pricing.
- Pay-to-rank that resembles an independent recommendation.
- A commission on work paid off-platform when Workdoe cannot verify the amount.
- Subscription-only access before a contractor sees that the local board has relevant volume.

### Pricing experiments, not promises

Run willingness-to-pay interviews and fake-door tests before billing. A suitable test sequence is:

1. Show a free contractor the exact Pro tools and a proposed monthly price.
2. Record intent without collecting payment.
3. Compare a low fixed subscription against a successful-match fee.
4. Ask contractors to calculate acceptable acquisition cost from their actual close rate and gross margin.
5. Do not use competitor anecdotes as a price anchor.

## Trust and Reputation

### Verification labels

Use atomic labels rather than one broad "verified" badge:

- Email confirmed
- Identity check completed
- Business registration checked
- License status checked, jurisdiction, type, and date
- Insurance document reviewed, coverage date
- Background check completed, provider and scope disclosed
- Workdoe project completed

No label should promise skill, safety, legality, or future performance beyond the exact check.

### Reviews

Reviews should open only after an approved Workdoe match is marked complete. Publish the review policy and treat positive and negative reviews consistently. The FTC's platform guidance recommends processes to verify genuine customer experience, transparent collection/moderation/publication, and investigation of reported fake reviews. The FTC rule effective October 21, 2024 prohibits specified fake, purchased, insider, suppressed, and manipulated reviews.

Pilot review design:

- one review per side per completed project;
- no star score until there is enough volume to avoid misleading precision;
- structured dimensions: communication, scope accuracy, timeliness, and work outcome;
- narrative text with report and response paths;
- no incentive conditioned on sentiment;
- display whether the project was platform-verified;
- preserve an audit trail for moderation changes.

### Safety operations

Workdoe needs a human operating loop before an open launch:

- report intake for user, project, bid, message, photo, and review;
- severity levels and response targets;
- emergency language that directs immediate danger to 911 rather than Workdoe;
- suspension and appeal states;
- duplicate/fraud/contactability tracking;
- evidence retention rules;
- law-enforcement request process;
- monitored support, privacy, security, and admin inboxes;
- incident and account-takeover runbooks.

## Login and Account Design

Same-domain email code sign-in is appropriate for pilot friction, but manually entered OTP is not phishing-resistant under NIST SP 800-63B. Workdoe should:

- keep Clerk UI embedded or custom-rendered on `workdoe.com`;
- use a production Clerk instance before inviting public accounts;
- make every code single-use and short-lived;
- rate-limit send and verify attempts;
- avoid revealing whether an email already exists;
- add optional passkeys after first successful authentication;
- require stronger authentication for admins and sensitive changes;
- log session revocation and webhook-driven account status;
- keep the permanent consumer/contractor role separation already selected.

- Seed only clearly labeled demonstration projects; never imply sample demand is live.

Do not open two broad zones on the same day. Start with a compact DC/Arlington-
Alexandria apartment-turnover corridor for the five-service lane above, but activate a
contractor only in jurisdictions where Workdoe has checked the applicable business and

claim that those jurisdictions share one legal regime.

Open a suburban cell in Bethesda/Silver Spring or Fairfax only after interviews identify

complete. Montgomery and Fairfax have substantial homeowner density, but their
home-improvement requirements make them poor places to improvise category rules.

1. At least three eligible contractors, plus one operational backup.
2. A reviewed allowed-scope and excluded-scope statement.
3. The required business, license, insurance, transport, or disposal evidence path.
4. A service-specific intake question set tested with at least five real project examples.
5. A monitored moderation and safety escalation owner.
6. A response-time promise supported by observed pilot data.

Do not open every taxonomy service just because the schema supports it. Expand one
service-zone cell at a time after the prior cell meets the response, match, completion,
and safety thresholds.

- Add reusable project templates and recurring schedules.

### Competitive interview prompts

Do not ask whether a contractor "likes Workdoe." Ask for comparable behavior:

1. Which channels produced the last ten won jobs, and what did each cost in cash and time?
2. Which lead details determine whether the contractor responds without a site visit?
3. What is the smallest profitable job after travel, parking, materials, and platform cost?
4. How often are current marketplace leads unreachable, duplicated, miscategorized, or already filled?
5. Would a visible four-bid maximum change willingness to respond, and why?
6. Which existing tool owns estimates, scheduling, invoices, and payments today?
7. Which Workdoe-only workflow would be worth paying for after the marketplace proves volume?

Consumer interviews should test the actual six-step post, profile comparison, and bid
approval. Ask participants to explain what each trust label proves, what remains unknown,
and when they expect an exact address, direct contact, payment, guarantee, or platform
intervention.

## Marketplace KPIs

### North-star pilot metric

**Qualified match rate:** percentage of published real projects that receive at least one eligible bid and result in a consumer-approved match within the promised time window.

For launch operations, report this metric by `service_slug x zone x week`. A healthy
regional average can hide a dead category or an unsafe jurisdiction. Also report the
number of eligible contractors before the first bid so marketing volume is not mistaken
for supply liquidity.

**Implementation snapshot, 2026-08-17.** The local app and Cloudflare Worker now
share a deterministic rolling 12-week admin projection for canonical service by
coarse zone by Monday week. It shows explicit published, brief-ready, with-bid,
matched, verified, controlled close-out, open-project-report, median-first-bid,
and current-supply denominators; keeps currently active cells visible at zero
projects; excludes local demo accounts; and emits no identity, contact, address,
narrative, raw timestamp, or project-row data. The current-supply number is a
review-time snapshot, so it must not be described as supply before first bid until
an approved historical snapshot is implemented. The response measure is the
first accepted Workdoe bid recorded for the project, not an appointment or proof
that the contractor remained eligible at every earlier moment.

Uber's official full-year 2025 and first-quarter 2026 reporting continues to
separate marketplace activity measures such as trips and monthly active platform
consumers from Gross Bookings and revenue. The useful inference for Workdoe is to
prove completed marketplace outcomes before selecting a revenue model. Workdoe
should not label posted budgets as Gross Bookings while it does not process or
observe the final customer payment.

### Funnel metrics

| Stage | Metric | Pilot interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Project start-to-publish conversion | Measures form burden and consumer intent |
| Quality | Scope completeness and moderation pass rate | Measures whether a contractor can act without repeated clarification |
| Supply | Eligible contractors per project | Measures local category liquidity, not raw signup count |
| Response | Time to first eligible bid | Measures practical usefulness |
| Alert response | Saved-view alert queue-to-bid conversion and time to bid | Tests whether opt-in fit alerts improve response without creating indiscriminate lead competition |
| Competition | Median qualified bids and cap utilization | Detects thin or wasteful competition |
| Match | Consumer approval rate | Tests bid relevance and trust |
| Fulfillment | Two-sided verified-completion rate | Tests marketplace truth beyond messages without treating every closed lead as completed work |
| Reliability | Cancellation/no-response/dispute rate by side | Identifies policy and matching failures |
| Retention | Repeat consumer post and contractor active-week rate | Tests recurring value |
| Repeat relationship | Invitation-to-fresh-bid rate and invited-contractor verified-completion rate | Tests whether continuity creates fulfilled work without mistaking an invitation or rebid for retention |
| Economics | Contractor-reported acquisition cost and gross profit after match | Required before monetization |
| Safety | Reports per 100 matches, severity, and resolution time | Required before opening more categories |

### Proposed beta exit criteria

These are operating hypotheses to confirm with the owner, not industry standards:

- At least 100 real projects across the two zones.
- At least 70 percent receive one eligible bid within 24 hours.
- Median project receives 2-4 qualified bids, never an unlimited pool.
- At least 45 percent of published projects produce an approved match.
- At least 70 percent of approved matches receive both participant completion confirmations.
- Fewer than 10 percent of published projects are invalid, duplicate, unauthorized, wrong-category, or unreachable.
- Severe safety reports have a documented same-day response and no unresolved critical incident.
- At least 30 percent of active contractors return in four separate weeks.
- At least 20 percent of consumers who need another service create a repeat project or template.

Do not commercialize merely because signup volume is high. Commercial launch requires evidence of completed local exchange.

## Product Roadmap From the Study

### Research-to-build update, 2026-08-17

The first repeat-provider experiment is now implemented in the local reference
app and Cloudflare Worker. A consumer can invite the contractor from a mutually
verified completed match to consider a new project for the same canonical
service. The contractor can pass or submit a fresh mini bid. The invitation
does not reserve a slot, bypass the service-zone gate, copy old terms, open a
message thread, or create an approval. Its schema stores relationship IDs,
service, status, and timestamps only; prior location, media, messages, contact
data, and bid terms stay out. This tests continuity demand without adopting a
membership or recurring-booking model before the DMV pilot has completion and
retention evidence.

The pilot can now observe this experiment without a third-party analytics
identity. Both implementations derive invitation state, fresh-bid conversion,
and invited-contractor verified completion from the marketplace ledger and show
recent project-level invitations to administrators. The production Worker also
queues one same-domain transactional alert to the invited contractor. That
message deliberately excludes the prior scope, ZIP/address, client identity,
contact details, messages, media, and bid terms. An email failure leaves the
private dashboard invitation intact and creates a redacted operational event.

The first contractor alert experiment is also implemented in the local
reference and Cloudflare release path. A contractor can save a category/area
view and explicitly opt into email. New-project fanout remains narrower than a
typical paid-lead blast: the Worker rechecks active availability, exact
canonical service capability, exact DMV zone, category/query fit, an open bid
window, and absence of an existing bid. One contractor/project delivery row
prevents routine duplication. The email reveals the new title, service, and
city/state only and routes the contractor to Workdoe to inspect remaining bid
slots. Pilot reporting should compare alert-to-bid conversion and time to first
eligible bid against contractors who use the board without email before any
alert, lead, or subscription monetization is proposed.

The completion-based reputation experiment is now implemented in the local
reference app and Cloudflare Worker. This is deliberately not a public star
race. Each active participant may leave one review only after an approved match
has mutual completion confirmation. The review uses four controlled dimensions
(communication, scope accuracy, timeliness, and work outcome), a controlled
work-together-again response, and optional capped narrative. The recipient may
respond once, either participant may report it once, and administrators can
hide/restore the review or resolve its report.

The contractor-profile projection is relationship-scoped and shows only
consumer-authored feedback from Workdoe-completed projects. It excludes client
identity, contact information, address/ZIP, media, and bid terms. Neither review
codes nor narrative affect search order, bid eligibility, account status,
payment, or paid placement. Automation events store only dimension codes,
roles, and narrative-presence booleans; they do not copy review, response, or
report text. Production still needs the policy approval, operator moderation
drill, and two-account acceptance evidence recommended by the FTC-aligned
design above.

The contractor storefront recommendation is now implemented in both the local
reference app and Cloudflare Worker:

- A seven-item readiness meter rewards useful profile evidence rather than
  points, rankings, or popularity.
- Canonical services and bounded DMV zones remain the matching source of truth.
- Work photos remain reportable and permission-checked; they cannot expose
  original storage keys or upload filenames.
- An optional business website must be a normalized public HTTPS URL. It is
  visible to the contractor, active administrators, and consumers evaluating
  that contractor's bid, but not to anonymous or unrelated visitors.
- The unused contractor phone field is no longer collected. Direct contact
  stays in the approved Workdoe message path.

This is consistent with current official service-marketplace guidance without
copying protected interface assets. Google tells service-area businesses to
hide an address when customers do not visit the location, choose explicit job
types, and define practical city/county/ZIP service areas. Taskrabbit describes
business photos as a fit signal and prohibits private-property violations,
personal contact details, and off-platform promotion in those photos. Workdoe's
own rule is stricter for outbound links: the business website is scoped to a
real bid relationship and labeled as contractor-provided.

### Lead-truth update, 2026-08-17

Current public competitor policies reinforce that a marketplace must measure
what happened to a request instead of treating every closed lead as completed
work:

- Porch publishes separate invalid-lead categories for bad contact data,
  solicitation, duplicates, miscategorization, requests outside a service
  area, stale demand, a consumer who did not request help, and a consumer who
  cannot authorize the work. It also distinguishes a quick consumer
  cancellation from an unresponsive request and from a contractor simply not
  winning the job.
- Bark states that nonresponse alone does not prove a bad lead, while incorrect
  contact details, duplicates, and fraudulent requests may be reportable. It
  directs professionals to keep service, location, and lead preferences
  accurate.
- Taskrabbit's cancellation flow asks the client to select a reason. Its
  current policy may charge for cancellation or unavailability within 24 hours
  because a worker reserved time and may have turned down other work.
- Angi's current contractor guidance emphasizes lead prequalification and fast
  response. That supports measuring scope readiness and response time, but it
  does not justify Workdoe manufacturing urgency or ranking contractors by who
  clicks first.

Workdoe now applies those general lessons without copying competitor code,
assets, copy, or commercial terms:

1. Closing a project requires one controlled outcome code: matched through
   Workdoe, hired elsewhere, plans changed, no bid fit, scope changed,
   duplicate, or other.
2. Only `workdoe-match` can enter two-sided completion confirmation. An
   approved lead that closes for another reason does not become contractor work
   history.
3. A contractor who submitted a bid may record one updateable lead-quality
   signal: insufficient detail, wrong service, outside service area, client
   unresponsive, already hired, duplicate, authorization concern, suspicious,
   or other.
4. Optional notes are capped, private to the submitting participant and active
   administrators, and excluded from automation-event payloads.
5. D1 stores the controlled codes as operational facts. No embedding,
   generative classifier, popularity score, or paid ranking changes a project's
   category, outcome, or contractor eligibility.
6. Four-question, versioned quote-readiness sets now exist for fourteen
   researched service lanes. Consumers may answer only controlled choices;
   clients and contractors see human-readable labels, while SQLite/D1 retain
   normalized codes for later cohort analysis. This implements the product
   scaffold, but each lane still needs the planned five-example operator review
   and the cross-lane set still needs the fifty-description validation study
   before the questions can be treated as commercially validated.
7. A six-signal project brief projection is now implemented in local and Worker
   paths. It names service, description, controlled scope, setting, timing, and
   budget-or-photo presence; it excludes identity, contact, exact location,
   contractor fit, payment, review, and paid rank. Five or six signals display
   `Ready to quote`, but the result never changes visibility or eligibility.

These signals should be reviewed in aggregate and sampled by an operator. A
single contractor's feedback must not automatically penalize a consumer, and a
consumer close reason must not adjudicate a payment or performance dispute.

## Commercial Launch Risks

| Risk | Early warning | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thin category liquidity | Projects with zero eligible contractors | Open services zone by zone only after supply threshold |
| Bad or exploratory demand | Nonresponse, no authority to hire, vague scope | Six-step intake, verification, transparent readiness signals, moderation |
| Contractor distrust | Low repeat bidding, complaints about competition | Visible bid cap, no shared lead fee, transparent eligibility |
| License misrepresentation | Mismatched business name/status | Official lookup, timestamps, atomic labels, recheck cadence |
| Safety incident | Off-platform deposits, threats, property access concern | Private contact, reporting, warnings, suspension, human escalation |
| Reputation manipulation | Review bursts or connected accounts | Completion-gated reviews, fraud signals, audit trail, FTC-aligned policy |
| Geographic sprawl | Long travel and cancellations | Service-specific zones and travel preferences |
| Premature monetization | Revenue with falling match/completion | Gate billing on liquidity and fulfillment metrics |
| Platform overbuilding | CRM/payment work before matches exist | Sequence project quality, liquidity, and trust first |
| Legal ambiguity | Confusing marketplace/employer/contractor language | Counsel-approved terms and consistent product states |
| Worker-status or platform-role change | Workdoe begins controlling price, schedule, assignment, performance method, or payment while law and enforcement guidance change | Quarterly legal watch, product-control inventory, counsel review before managed fulfillment or billing |
| Misread substitute competition | Treating Facebook Marketplace item listings or raw neighborhood posts as equivalent service-marketplace demand | Measure actual project posts, eligible bids, and completed matches; use groups only as an interview/recruitment channel |

## Current Launch Readiness

The competitive research strengthens the product direction but does not change the current no-go decision for unrestricted public account creation.

Open blockers already documented in the repository:

1. Production still needs a proven Clerk production instance and `CLERK_WEBHOOK_SECRET` set.
2. Candidate Privacy, Terms, and Safety routes now exist, including minimum-age and prohibited-work language. Operator identity, monitored contact ownership, retention/deletion decisions, binding acceptance, and final legal approval remain open; candidate copy is not legal approval.
3. A live consumer OTP -> project -> contractor bid -> approval -> message -> two-sided completion -> two-sided feedback/response/report -> admin moderation journey has not been completed with two real accounts.
4. Production recovery, credential rotation, deletion/export, incident, and moderation drills remain incomplete.
5. Manual screen-reader, keyboard, authenticated mobile, and independent security testing remain open.
6. Workdoe's original source does not yet have a selected top-level license. Open-source dependencies are documented, but the Workdoe repository itself must not be called open source until a license is chosen.
7. Qualified counsel has not yet reviewed Workdoe's actual marketplace controls against current worker-classification, employment-service, tax-reporting, principal/agent, and consumer-contract requirements.

The contractor credential claim ledger is implemented and tested locally and
in the Cloudflare Worker. It closes the schema gap identified by this study,
but it does not establish that a contractor is legally eligible for a specific
project. The controlled beta must prove its human review, expiry, recheck, and
escalation procedure with real official sources before inviting contractors to
rely on the label.

The correct next release is a controlled, invite-only DMV beta after these gates close, not an unrestricted commercial launch.
